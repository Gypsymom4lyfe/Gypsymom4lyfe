## 🎯 Making a Strong Inquiry

### **For Infrastructure Partnerships:**

**Strong inquiry includes:**
- Geographic region and specific corridors of interest
- Existing utility/rail partnerships
- Broadband coverage gaps and community served
- Timeline for deployment
- Budget considerations
- Key decision-makers and stakeholders

**Example:**
> "We represent the Anytown Regional Broadband Authority serving rural Virginia. We have unserved communities along the CSX rail corridor, approximately 45 miles of accessible right-of-way, and $5M in BEAD funding. We're interested in a pilot T-Core deployment to serve last-mile broadband to 8 municipalities. Can we discuss timeline and technical customization for our corridor?"

---

### **For Workforce Partnerships:**

**Strong inquiry includes:**
- Educational institution name and type (community college, high school, workforce development board)
- Current programs and capacity
- Number of potential students (this year and 3-year projection)
- Instructor and lab facility status
- Regional employers interested in hiring graduates
- Timeline for program launch

**Example:**
> "Tidewater Community College is interested in implementing the Digital Twin Curriculum starting Fall 2026. We have 3 qualified instructors, a 2,000 sq ft lab facility, and preliminary interest from 25+ high school dual-enrollment students. We've also connected with CSX and several regional utilities interested in hiring certified operators. Can we discuss customization for our region?"

---

### **For Community Application Partnerships:**

**Strong inquiry includes:**
- Organization type and mission
- Community being served (population size, demographics)
- Specific need/problem being addressed
- User personas and scenarios
- Rough timeline and resource availability
- Interest in available infrastructure or deployment questions

**Example:**
> "We're a environmental non-profit working with underserved communities in the Chesapeake Bay region. We're interested in building a community science platform similar to ForKids' TOKA Explorer to engage youth in water quality monitoring. We have 3 technical staff and $100k budget for year 1. What infrastructure is available in our region? How would we integrate our data with the larger ecosystem?"

## 🚀 Post-Inquiry Next Steps

### **If Your Inquiry is Accepted for Partnership:**

1. **Feasibility Assessment** (Weeks 1-2)
   - Technical/operational assessment
   - Preliminary resource estimates
   - Risk and mitigation planning

2. **Phase 1 Planning** (Weeks 2-4)
   - Detailed project planning
   - Budget and timeline confirmation
   - Team assignments
   - Partnership agreement preparation

3. **Formal Agreement** (Weeks 4-6)
   - Legal review
   - Stakeholder alignment
   - Signature and execution

4. **Kickoff** (Weeks 6+)
   - Project initiation
   - Onboarding of partner team
   - Work begins

### **If Your Inquiry Doesn't Fit Current Capacity:**

You will receive:
- Clear explanation of why
- Suggestions for alternative approaches
- Potential future timing or scaling opportunities
- Connection to similar projects or resources
